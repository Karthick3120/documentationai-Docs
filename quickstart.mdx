## Overview

This quickstart helps you turn a blank documentation space into a structured, publishable site. You will create your first page, organize sections, add placeholders for future content, and prepare the site for sharing with your team.

<Callout kind="info">

This is a starter kit template. Use it as a foundation, then adapt the examples, labels, and sections to match your product and workflow.

</Callout>

## Set up your first docs structure

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create your homepage" icon="book-open">
    Create a page that welcomes readers, explains the docs purpose, and links to the next most useful sections.

    Keep the first version short and focused. A clear homepage makes the rest of the site easier to navigate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add sections and navigation" icon="settings">
    Use headings to split your content into logical sections such as Overview, Setup, Examples, and Next Steps.

    Then add links in your navigation so readers can move between related topics without guessing where to go next.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Insert content placeholders" icon="code">
    Add short placeholder paragraphs, sample lists, and example blocks where future content will live.

    This helps your team see the intended structure before every page is fully written.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish your site" icon="rocket">
    Review the page, confirm the links work, and publish the documentation site when the structure feels ready.

    You can improve content over time without changing the overall layout.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Copy a simple starter page

Use the example below as a starting point for your own docs. It shows a clean structure with a title, a short summary, and a few practical sections.

<CodeGroup tabs="Markdown,MDX">
  ```markdown
  ## Getting started

  Welcome to the project docs. This page explains the basics and helps you find the next steps.

  ### Before you begin

  - Decide what this page should teach
  - Link to the most important related pages
  - Keep the first draft short and clear

  ### Next steps

  1. Add an example
  2. Add a troubleshooting note
  3. Publish the page
  ```

## Choose the right setup for your team

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Small team" icon="users">
    Keep the site compact. Start with a homepage, one getting-started page, and a few focused guides.

    This approach works well when you want to ship quickly and refine the structure later.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Growing team" icon="bar-chart">
    Create a clear section for each major topic, then add navigation links between related pages.

    This makes it easier for new contributors to add content without breaking the overall structure.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Large project" icon="database">
    Split content into product docs, how-to guides, reference pages, and release notes.

    A deeper structure helps readers find the right level of detail without scanning long pages.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Recommended publishing checklist

Before you publish, confirm that the page is easy to scan and ready for real users.

- You have at least one useful starting page
- Your headings follow a clear order
- Your navigation points to the most important content
- Your placeholder text is obvious and easy to replace
- Your links open the right pages

<Callout kind="tip">

The best starter docs stay simple. You can always add advanced sections, examples, and diagrams after your first version is live.

</Callout>

## Next steps

Once this page is live, build out the rest of your documentation with the same pattern: one purpose, one audience, and one clear next action per page. That keeps your docs easy to grow and easy to maintain.

For example, you can add pages for setup instructions, core concepts, configuration details, and frequently asked questions.
